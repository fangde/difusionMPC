📖 Abstract

Generative models, notably Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) and Continuous Normalizing Flows via Flow Matching (CFM), have demonstrated remarkable efficacy in robot imitation learning and trajectory generation. However, both paradigms fundamentally rely on statistical conditioning: their velocity and score fields regress onto empirical averages of offline demonstrations. When applied to underactuated, dynamically constrained physical systems—such as an underactuated double pendulum with a single motor actuator—statistical interpolation routinely violates non-holonomic constraints and equations of motion, leading to control failure under perturbations. In this work, we propose Model-Driven Diffusion. Rather than matching empirical demonstration differences, Model-Driven Diffusion defines the generative drift velocity directly using Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Hamiltonian dynamics. We establish a unified vector-field framework comparing: (1) Score-based Diffusion, (2) Conditional Flow Matching, and (3) Model-Driven Diffusion under identical perturbation-recovery conditions from stable upright equilibria. Rigorous Monte Carlo benchmarking proves that Model-Driven Diffusion achieves near-optimal recovery success rates while completely eliminating the requisite offline training data.

Scientific Finding — The Physical Bound vs. The Data Bound:

Expanding data from 1,000 to 3,000 trajectories enables Flow Matching to reach 100% recovery at low noise, perfectly replicating the optimal controller. However, under extreme disturbances ($\sigma \ge 0.25\text{ rad}$), Flow Matching plateaus at exactly ~59% and ~38%, matching the physical ceiling of the model-driven controller.

This proves that the bottleneck at high noise is the physical basin of attraction of underactuated dynamics under torque saturation ($|u| \le 15\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$), rather than empirical dataset scarcity.

⚙️ Mathematical Formulation

The planar underactuated double pendulum (Pendubot) dynamics satisfy Euler-Lagrange equations:

$$M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q) = B u, \quad B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

where $q = [\theta_1, \theta_2]^T$ and single motor input is bounded by $|u| \le u_{max}$.

We cast the three paradigms into a unified continuous vector-field restoration process $\dot{x} = \mathbf{v}(x)$:

  • Method 1 (Score Diffusion): $\mathbf{v}_{Score}(x) = -\frac{1}{2}\beta x - \frac{1}{2}g^2 \nabla_x \log p(x)$ (Empirical statistical density score)
  • Method 2 (Flow Matching): $\mathbf{v}_{CFM}(x) = \mathbb{E}[x^* - x_{pert} \mid x]$ (Empirical transport average)
  • Method 3 (Model-Driven Diffusion): $\mathbf{v}_{MPC}(x) = f(x, u^*_{MPC}(x))$ (Physical optimal control dynamics)
